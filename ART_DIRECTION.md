# Footprints — visual continuity

The approved lower half of each original poster is the visual source of truth. Animate the existing material artwork; do not replace it with a photoreal landscape covered by a square grid.

- Preserve the most recognizable structure, perspective, proportions, balance and color atmosphere of the approved lower-half composition.
- Use chalky, matte shell/mineral fragments with natural layers, small chips and shallow relief. Keep a square-module foundation; subject-defining detail may use shaped fragments, as in the approved Seattle reference.
- Keep warm near-white paper visible. The overall subject plus dissolving fragments occupies roughly 60–70% of the composition; that percentage is NOT the whitespace requirement.
- Preserve dense material cores and increasingly sparse, missing, disconnected fragments at the edge. Avoid a uniformly tiled rectangle or an edge-to-edge landscape.
- Keep the high-key palette: pale sky blue, aqua, fresh greens, warm yellow, coral and soft pink accents. Avoid muddy aged colors, plastic gloss and strong pearlescence.
- No persistent labels over the map. Hover/focus highlights a small area with a light, unboxed English place name.
- Do not add unrelated human protagonists. Motion may belong to light, water, a leaf, an existing vessel, reeds, clouds, or reflections. Preserve any original people only if the reference and story actually call for them.
- Each scene has a small environmental event with change and resolution. Do not give every place the same generic person or activity.
- Treat these as fictional visual studies. Personal memory, emotion and narrative fields remain empty until the user supplies them.

The early instruction's very low module-count limit was followed by an explicitly preferred Seattle reference with much richer detail. Match that accepted reference's recognizable structure and material density; do not simplify it into a handful of blocks, and do not claim an exact fragment count without measuring it.

Animation may show slight whole-frame motion; the user accepted a stop-motion demo. Avoid spending effort polishing one scene while leaving other destinations unfinished.

Every story frame must now be a separately generated native image approximately 1448×1086 (4:3), with only minor native dimension variation (minimum 1440×1080). Never upscale or crop the old six-panel sheets and call them HD. Validate actual dimensions before integration. The story viewport is at most 1000 CSS pixels wide.

Seattle's new story is an explicit exception to the usual quiet framing: the original large cargo ship approaches from the distant harbor, grows with plausible perspective while the skyline remains fixed, and finally fills/crops beyond the animation viewport. Only the ship and its associated water/reflection change scale. Do not substitute a camera zoom, a moving little ferry, an explosion or an invented human protagonist. Paper margins may be occluded by the looming ship in the final frames.
